## What was built

**The acceptance bar** is organised as a job map read off the two files that already decide what users get, `core/registry/catalog.json` and `core/registry/recommended.json`, rather than from an invented list. 41 jobs: 18 default jobs pre-ticked per hardware tier, 20 opt-in jobs, 3 runtime jobs. Every one of the 38 catalog ids holds exactly one job, so none needed the open-question fallback. The bar itself is two routes and no others: take a job with measured local numbers, or claim a job nobody holds. The license section states a revealed policy rather than a new one, anchored on the v2.4.10 decision that routing low-VRAM users to a revenue-capped license is a harm worth a release to fix.

Five open questions are recorded rather than papered over. The load-bearing two are a genuine two-sided divergence between the registries: eight `ModelCatalog.ts` format bindings have no installable catalog entry, and five installable LLMs have no format binding, three of them the pre-ticked chat default on the cpu, 12/16 and 24 GB tiers. `inkling-small` is the sharp end -- `family: "inkling"`, `agentic: true`, and no binding, so `parseAgentToolCalls` falls back to `"gemma4-xml"` and parses its tool calls with a Gemma grammar. Nothing tests the two registries against each other, which is why both were invisible.

**The critic** carries two load-bearing properties in the definition itself: it never authorizes an irreversible or outward-facing action, and an unaccounted "looks good" is a failed review. Its verdict format makes a `NOT ASSESSED` list mandatory, because a verdict listing no unexamined dimensions is claiming total coverage of a real change range.

## Deviation

**One addition beyond the plan text.** The plan's 1.4 asked for the catalog-vs-job-map check to be recorded in the evidence file. It was, but the check was also promoted from a scratch script into a committed meta-test, `tests/unit/docs/v2.4.9-model-acceptance.test.ts` (5 assertions). Reasons: the repository already carries seven doc-contract meta-tests under `tests/unit/docs/`, so this follows an established convention rather than introducing one; and the bar names this exact failure ("A job that quietly acquires a second holder is the specific failure this document exists to catch"), which an unrun scratch script cannot catch. The test was proven to fail before being trusted -- a second holder was injected, two of five assertions caught it by name, and the injection was reverted. No `T` id covers it; it is recorded here and in the CI-impact record.

## Plan delta

**Disposition: False assumption.** Non-blocking.

**Observed evidence.** The plan's Constitution Check opened "Four `CLAUDE.md` rules bind this plan and are treated as gates", and sub-task 1.2 instructed that the agent carry `CLAUDE.md` as the first of its documented standards. There is no `CLAUDE.md` in this repository:

```
$ ls CLAUDE.md
ls: cannot access 'CLAUDE.md': No such file or directory
$ git ls-files | grep -i 'CLAUDE.md'
(empty)
```

The absence is deliberate and asserted. `AGENTS.md`'s opening section states "there is no `CLAUDE.md`, no Claude-specific instructions, no Anthropic-bound assumptions in product files", and `tests/unit/docs/AGENTS-md.test.ts:48` is a meta-test named "CLAUDE.md is removed (Nexus uses agent-agnostic naming)" asserting `existsSync(claudeMdPath)` is false.

The assumption was followed into the deliverable before it was caught: the agent definition shipped citing `../../CLAUDE.md` as "the first of the only authority you cite", and the standards judge itself surfaced this on its first invocation, as both a `CONFLICTS` entry against its own definition and an `[optimization]` finding against the identical misattribution in the sibling v2.4.10 plan. The critic catching a defect in the phase that created it is the strongest evidence available that the 1.2 design works.

**Consequence for the remaining phases.** The four rules are real and the gate still binds; only the attribution was wrong, and AGENTS.md `## Critical Rules` carries all four verbatim. So this changes no phase's scope. Two things follow:

1. Corrected in place, four occurrences: the v2.4.9 Constitution Check, the 1.2 failure-mode list, the 1.2 prompt, and the sentence the v2.5.0 plan inherited. Each now names AGENTS.md `## Critical Rules` and states that the absence of `CLAUDE.md` is deliberate.
2. **Phases 2 through 5 must cite AGENTS.md, never `CLAUDE.md`,** in any evidence file, commit message, or generated artifact. The judge treats the misattribution as a reportable finding, so a later phase that reintroduces it will be caught by its own gate rather than at review.

Not escalated to a known gap or a plan-scope change: the rules bind either way, and the fix was a four-line attribution correction rather than a change to what any phase does.
