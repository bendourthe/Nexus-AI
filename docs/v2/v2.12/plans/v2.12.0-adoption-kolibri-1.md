## Overview

A social post presented Kolibri-1 (Aleph Alpha, released 2026-10-03, Apache-2.0) as a model "to run solid reasoning locally on your machine" with a 1M-token context. The seeding comparison read the first-party model card and found otherwise: 78B total parameters (3.46B active), about 78 GB of FP8 weights, a stated floor of two A100 80 GB cards or one H100-class GPU, a trained context of 262,144 tokens (1M is an extrapolation the vendor advises against in production), and no support in stock Ollama, llama.cpp, or LM Studio. Every community GGUF needs a patched llama.cpp, and the smallest one (Q3_K_S) is roughly 34 GB by arithmetic, above the 24 GB top tier in `core/registry/recommended.json`. The model fails gates 3, 4, and 5 of the v2.3.0 admission record.

The decline itself follows the convention every declined model has used since v2.8.0: one known-gap row and one guard test, with the acceptance bar kept free of the name. The review found that convention leaves a gap: each record lives in a release-scoped file that moves into `docs/archive/` when its release closes, so the maintainer who checks a newly hyped model has nowhere obvious to look. This plan therefore also adds a living index of declined proposals under `docs/reference/`, backfilled with the five earlier declines, and links it from the acceptance bar without naming any declined model there.

Success means that the next time Kolibri-1 or any earlier declined model is proposed, the maintainer opens one living page linked from the acceptance bar, finds the decision and its reopen conditions, and that no catalog edit can quietly admit Kolibri-1 without failing a test.
