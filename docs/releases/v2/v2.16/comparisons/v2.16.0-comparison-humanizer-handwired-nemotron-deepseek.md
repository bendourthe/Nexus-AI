## Facts established from primary sources

### Nemotron 3.5 family (Source 4)

- **License**: OpenMDW-1.1 on every Bolt card. The catalog already ships OpenMDW-1.1 for `nemotron-lightning:30b-a3b` and its offload sibling (`core/registry/catalog.json`), so the license posture is settled precedent, not a new decision.
- **Super VL**: 120B total, 12B active, vision. Official Ollama tag `nemotron-3.5-super:120b`. A 120B model at any interactive quantization is several times the 24 GB tier.
- **Bolt 74B-A9B**: Mamba-2/Transformer hybrid MoE, 74B total, 9B active, native IQ2 (about 2.06 bits per expert weight). Text, image, and video input; 256K context; thinking toggle via `enable_thinking`; OpenAI-style tool calls, served by vLLM with the `qwen3_coder` parser. Official Ollama library tag `nemotron-3.5-bolt:74b-a9b` (aliases `latest`, `74b-a9b-iq2`, one digest), **25 GB on disk**, vision, tools, and thinking listed. The card itself is internally inconsistent (Safetensors metadata reports 22B parameters; the hardware list names both A100/H100 and only Blackwell SM120/121).
- **Bolt E4B**: about 4 GB, aimed at conversational and on-device agents, announced for 2026-10-23. No card, no Ollama tag, no license text yet.
- Vendor benchmarks (PinchBench, BrowseComp, throughput) do not count toward any job under the acceptance bar and are not repeated here.

### DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017 (Source 5)

- MIT on the official card (one secondary outlet reported the 1017 license as undisclosed; the card settles it).
- 763B total parameters, 1M context, image-text-to-text. No official Ollama tag; only community quantizations, and the official checkpoint is roughly 510 GB.
- No concrete numbers behind the "faster, shorter outputs" claim on either the card or the announcement.
- Search and Fetch APIs: USD 0.75 per 1,000 calls, free through 2026-10-31 16:00 UTC when called from DeepSeek Harness, Claude Code, or Codex.

### Humanizer (Source 1)

- Fine-tune of `google/gemma-4-12B` (SFT, DPO, three GRPO rounds with an LLM fact judge). Card states Apache-2.0.
- GGUF from 3.9 GB (IQ2_XS-QAT) to 12.7 GB (Q8_0); Ollama needs a custom Modelfile and a raw text-completion prompt rather than the chat template.
- Fact preservation: 376/420 English and 149/204 Chinese rewrites judged free of factual problems by another LLM. So roughly 1 in 10 English rewrites and 1 in 4 Chinese rewrites changed a fact. The card itself tells users to proofread numbers, dates, and names.
- Headline evaluation is Originality.ai pass rate (95% judged human).

### Handwired (Sources 2 and 3)

- MIT, Bun-based, about 1.9k stars. Compiles branchy JavaScript into matmul-plus-ReLU form so a program is differentiable; runs on CPU or WebGPU. Loop and state handling, supported subset, and the LLM-mixing idea are not documented beyond a proposed nanoGPT experiment. The `lab/` folder and README tone mark it as research.

## Per-source analysis against Nexus

### 4a. Super VL: decline

Fails the single-GPU ceiling (STRATEGY.md section 3, principle 3) on the same ground as Kolibri-1 in [declined-models.md](../../../../reference/declined-models.md). The only seat it could argue for, a patient-tier disk-offload frontier MoE, is held by `inkling-small` (74.8 GB UD-IQ1_S), and Super VL brings no local numbers to take it.

**Reopen when**: an official Ollama tag fits a 24 GB GPU, or a measured disk-offload run beats `inkling-small` on the same task set.

### 4b. Bolt 74B-A9B: measure before admitting

Bolt is the only source that clears the license, runtime, and vision-delivery gates on day one: OpenMDW-1.1 is already in the catalog, and the official library tag lists vision, which satisfies the "Reopening the bar" rule in model-acceptance.md (vision through an official tag, never an `hf.co` pull with a separate `mmproj`).

It still has no job to claim. Each candidate seat is occupied:

| Job (model-acceptance.md) | Holder | What Bolt would have to show |
|---|---|---|
| 24 GB MoE worker candidate, native build (opt-in) | `nemotron-lightning:30b-a3b` | A paired A/B on the 28 golden tasks, run the same way as the v2.14.0 LFM move ([lfm-ab-results.json](../../../../archives/v2/v2.14/development/lfm-ab-results.json)): completion, p95 turn time, RAM spill. This is the natural seat: same vendor, same license, same 25 GB on disk, with vision and 256K context added. |
| 24 GB chat-and-agentic default (pre-ticked) | `gemma4:31b` | The higher bar: peak memory, tokens per second, a local tool-call transcript, and a reason to displace a holder that already covers chat and agentic in one download. "Has vision" is not that reason, because the Gemma 4 family already accepts images. |
| 24 GB coding-specialist fallback | `qwen3-coder:30b` | Same as above, plus beating the Gemma variant. |

Two facts make the measurement non-trivial:

1. **25 GB does not fit in 24 GB of VRAM** before any KV cache. Ollama will spill layers to system RAM. `nemotron-lightning:30b-a3b` is also 25 GB and holds its 24 GB job anyway, so this is not disqualifying, but the A/B must record spill the way the LFM A/B did.
2. **Runtime binding cost.** `ModelCatalog.ts` binds Lightning to `promptFormat: "qwen"`, `toolFormat: "qwen-json"`. Bolt's card names the `qwen3_coder` parser on vLLM, which is a different wire format. Whether Ollama's template hides that difference, and which existing `TOOL_FORMAT_NAMES` parser reads Bolt's output, must be proven on a captured local transcript. OQ-6 (`minicpm5:2b`, whose tool delimiters were tokenizer special tokens Ollama omitted) is the failure this guards against. A new family string also hits OQ-4: the `ModelFamily` union and its hand-maintained copies change.

**If the A/B wins**: Bolt takes the 24 GB MoE worker job and `nemotron-lightning:30b-a3b` becomes a removal candidate (the offload sibling keeps its distinct 1M-context-on-16 GB job). This is a job move, not an addition. **If no 24 GB host is available**: Bolt stays out. The bar allows an unmeasured opt-in only for an uncovered job, and "vision MoE worker at 24 GB" is a relabelling of an occupied seat.

### 4c. Bolt E4B: watch, then measure first

This is the item with the most user value, because of a commitment the project has already made. `lfm2.5:2.6b` holds the cpu-tier dedicated agentic default (and is inserted at 8 GB) under the LFM Open License v1.0, whose `licenseNote` caps free commercial use at USD 10M annual revenue. The acceptance bar calls a commercially capped license "not acceptable for a pre-ticked default when an alternative fits the same tier", and OQ-6 records that the last attempted replacement (`minicpm5:2b`) failed because its tool calls never survived Ollama's detokenizer.

A 4 GB OpenMDW-1.1 agent model could discharge that commitment. Nothing about it can be verified until it ships. **Gates, in order**:

1. Official Ollama library tag exists, with tools listed.
2. License text on the card reads OpenMDW-1.1 (or another non-capped license).
3. A local transcript proves tool calls arrive intact through Ollama and parse with an existing `TOOL_FORMAT_NAMES` parser. This is the OQ-6 gate and the one most likely to fail.
4. Peak resident memory against the cpu and 8 GB budgets, tokens per second on CPU, and the golden-task A/B against `lfm2.5:2.6b`.

Gates 1 and 2 are checks of a web page; gate 3 is an afternoon on any machine. Run them in that order and stop at the first failure.

### 5. DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017: decline; APIs are a hard no

- **Model**: 763B total with no official Ollama tag. Even the most aggressive community quantization of a roughly 510 GB checkpoint is many times the 24 GB tier, so it fails the single-GPU ceiling and the official-tag rule. The DeepSWE score is the vendor's own and would not count toward any job regardless. Same grounds as Kolibri-1.
- **Search and Fetch APIs**: search-as-service and scraping-as-service, both named hard-no categories in the MCP Registry Policy (AGENTS.md / README) and a STRATEGY.md non-goal ("any runtime dependency on a third-party ... search service"). The "free when called via Claude Code" promotion changes the price, not the data flow: every query would leave the machine.

**Reopen when**: DeepSeek publishes a distilled or small Flash variant with an official Ollama tag that fits 24 GB.

### 1. Humanizer: decline as a catalog entry

- **No job.** It is a fine-tune of the same Gemma 4 12B base that `gemma-4-12b-it-gguf` ships as the 12 and 16 GB chat-and-agentic default. "Rewrite this draft so it reads naturally" is a prompt to that default, not a new seat. Adding it costs a second 7.6 to 12.7 GB download for one task.
- **Runtime fit.** No official Ollama tag; it needs an `hf.co` pull with a custom Modelfile and a raw completion prompt. Every LLM in the catalog uses an official library tag.
- **License label risk.** The card says Apache-2.0; the catalog records the Gemma 4 family under "Gemma Terms of Use". A derivative's license must be checked against its base before it is labelled. The acceptance bar names a wrong license label as the failure that defeats the invariant test. Not resolved here.
- **Purpose.** The card leads with Originality.ai pass rates. Detector evasion is not a Nexus user need, and STRATEGY.md metric M5 (a capability the maintainer actually uses within two cycles, or it is removed) argues against shipping it.
- **What remains possible**: a user who wants it can pull it into their own Ollama; runtime model discovery (`desktop/sidecar/src/models/modelsService.ts`) will list it without a catalog row.
- **Runtime fit, already measured**: the v2.14.1 feasibility record found that the model's required controls (raw completion prompt, `min_p`, repetition penalty, stop/EOS override, output-token bound) have no typed field in `modules/coding/llm/types.ts`. That finding still holds and is independent of the card.

**The fact-preservation idea is already built.** v2.14.1 item H2 shipped `scripts/evaluate-local-rewrites.mjs`, which checks numbers with units, dates, names, exact quotes, negation, links, code, and tables across a frozen English and Chinese corpus. The first draft of this report proposed it as new; it is not. Its only open question is `DF-8` (live rewrite quality unmeasured after the pilot's format HOLD), which needs a new preregistered experiment, not this source.

### 2/3. Handwired: not applicable

Nexus is a local generative studio, not a differentiable-programming platform. The demos map onto nothing the persona needs:

- The 100k-document JSON search demo overlaps with retrieval Nexus already has (`core/codegraph/` SQLite FTS5, `DenseIndex` / `PrunedDenseIndex`), and nothing in the known gaps reports retrieval speed as a problem.
- Training "parts of regular programs" or wiring a frozen program into a network has no current Nexus feature to serve.
- It is a Bun project with a `lab/` folder of experiments and no stated stability.

No adoption. The v2.14.1 comparison reached the same result in more detail (W1 conditional testing practice, W2 and W3 skip, W4 future), and no Nexus need has appeared since. Worth re-reading only if Nexus ever ships a WebGPU compute path in the renderer, which is not on any plan.

## Prioritized adoption sequence

Ordered per the reverse-engineer-first rule; there are no `skill-native` items worth shipping now and no `vendor-intrinsic` items.

1. **After 2026-10-23: Bolt E4B gates 1 and 2** (page checks). Stop if either fails and add a declined-models row.
2. **Bolt E4B gate 3**: capture a local Ollama transcript and run it through the existing parsers. Stop and record against OQ-6 if no parser returns calls.
3. **Bolt E4B gate 4**: memory, CPU throughput, and golden-task A/B against `lfm2.5:2.6b`. On a win, it takes the cpu-tier dedicated agentic job, gets a `ModelCatalog.ts` binding, and `model-acceptance.md`, `recommended.json`, and `catalog.json` change in one commit as that document requires.
4. **Bolt 74B-A9B A/B** against `nemotron-lightning:30b-a3b` on a 24 GB host, recording RAM spill and tool-call parsing. Independent of steps 1-3 and can run in parallel if hardware allows.
5. **Record the declines** (Super VL, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) in `docs/reference/declined-models.md`, and note on the Humanizer's v2.14.1 HOLD that the 2026-10-20 resubmission added no reopening evidence, with the reopen conditions above, so the next news item about these models resolves in one lookup.

Dependencies: none between steps 1-3 and step 4. Step 5 can land at any time.

## Risks and conflicts

- **Catalog seam (OQ-3, OQ-4)**: either Bolt admission adds an LLM to `catalog.json` and needs a matching `ModelCatalog.ts` entry; a new `ModelFamily` string means editing every hand-maintained copy of that union, and the root `tsc -b` does not typecheck `desktop/`.
- **Two-channel rule**: any admitted model must work in both the desktop sidecar and the VS Code extension.
- **Default-tier blast radius**: Bolt E4B replacing `lfm2.5:2.6b` changes what every cpu-tier and 8 GB install pre-ticks. That is the purpose, but it is a pre-ticked default and gets the full "taking a job" evidence bar, not the opt-in bar.
- **Announced-only facts**: E4B's size, license, and capabilities come from secondary coverage of an unreleased model. Nothing in this report about E4B should be cited as verified until its card exists.
