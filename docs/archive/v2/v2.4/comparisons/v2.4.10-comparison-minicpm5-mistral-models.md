## Section 1: Executive Summary

Two questions, two different answers.

**MiniCPM5-2B: yes, add it.** It lands directly in the one catalog slot that is currently filled by a license-compromised entry. The sub-4 GB agentic tier is held today by LFM2.5-2.6B, whose LFM Open License v1.0 caps free commercial use at USD 10M annual revenue. MiniCPM5-2B is Apache-2.0, is slightly smaller as a Q4_K_M GGUF (1.56 GB vs 1.67 GB), carries the same 128K context, is built on stock `LlamaForCausalLM` so llama.cpp and Ollama need no custom kernels, and ships an official ungated GGUF repo. It was released 2026-09-07, which makes it the newest model in the entire catalog. It is the first candidate in a while that improves a slot rather than crowding it.

**Mistral: one entry is defensible, the rest are not.** Mistral Small 4 is a 119B MoE and sits far outside the 24 GB hardware ceiling the catalog is built around. Devstral Small 2 (24B, coding) overlaps Qwen3-Coder 30B and, critically, has no first-party GGUF repository, only community re-uploads. Ministral 3 at 3B and 8B is redundant against MiniCPM5-2B, Qwen3.5 4B, and Qwen3.5 9B. That leaves **Ministral 3 14B Instruct**: Apache-2.0, official `mistralai` GGUF, ungated, 8.24 GB at Q4_K_M, 256K context, vision, native function calling. It is the only license-clean alternative to `gemma-4-12b-it-gguf` in the 11 to 14 GB band, where every current option is under the Gemma Terms of Use. Add it as an opt-in entry, not a tier default.

**Sorting: the two surfaces share a comparator but NOT its tier input.** This section originally claimed ordering was a solved problem needing no change. That was wrong for the installer, and the correction is recorded in Section 5 below. Desktop Settings reads a row's `tags` from `catalog.json`; the installer discards those tags and derives `recommended` from membership in `recommended.json`. Promotion therefore requires editing both files, or the two surfaces disagree.

**Total: 2 adoptions, 4 rejections, 1 corrected sorting claim, 3 blocking verification gates.**

> **Correction, 2026-09-09.** Sections 1, 5, 9, and 10 originally stated that display position is a pure function of `tags` and `releaseDate` on both surfaces and that no sorting work was needed. Review of `scripts/installer/src/nexus_installer/pages/typed_catalog.py` disproved that. The affected sections are corrected in place and marked. Section 9's plan is also superseded by decisions recorded in [the v2.4.10 adoption plan](../plans/v2.4.10-adoption-minicpm5-mistral-models.md): Ministral 3 14B is cut, coding-runtime registration is added, and promotion changes both tier inputs.

## Section 2: What the catalog looks like today

38 entries in `core/registry/catalog.json`, of which 15 are LLMs. The relevant subset:

| Slot | Current entry | License | Q4 size | VRAM | Context |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agentic, CPU / sub-4 GB | `lfm2.5:2.6b` | **LFM Open License v1.0** (USD 10M revenue cap) | 1.67 GB | 3 | 128K |
| Agentic, 4 GB | `qwen3.5:4b` | Apache-2.0 | 3.4 GB | 4 | 256K |
| Agentic, 8 GB | `qwen3.5:9b` | Apache-2.0 | 6.6 GB | 8 | 256K |
| Chat, 11 GB | `gemma-4-12b-it-gguf` | **Gemma Terms of Use** | 7.6 GB | 11 | 262K |
| Agentic, 16 GB | `gpt-oss:20b` | Apache-2.0 | 14 GB | 16 | 128K |
| Agentic, 20 GB | `qwen3-coder:30b` | Apache-2.0 | 19 GB | 20 | 256K |
| Chat, 22 GB | `gemma4:31b` | **Gemma Terms of Use** | 17.5 GB | 22 | n/a |

The hardware tiers the installer pre-ticks are `cpu`, `8`, `12`, `16`, `24` (`core/registry/recommended.json`). Nothing in the catalog exceeds 24 GB VRAM. That ceiling is the single most decisive filter applied below.

Two structural observations drive the recommendations:

1. **The two smallest and the two largest chat/agentic slots are the license-constrained ones.** LFM at the bottom, Gemma Terms of Use at 11 GB and 22 GB. Everything in between is already Apache-2.0.
2. **The catalog holds a hard provenance bar.** `scripts/installer/src/nexus_installer/catalog_invariants.py` bans known-broken Ollama pull targets by name (the Unsloth `hf.co` GGUF reference downloads fully then errors HTTP 400 on manifest commit), requires SHA256 pins per weight file, and forbids vendor benchmark numbers in card copy until locally reproduced. Any candidate without a first-party GGUF repo starts at a disadvantage.

## Section 3: Candidate 1 -- MiniCPM5-2B

**Verdict: ADOPT.**

| Attribute | MiniCPM5-2B | Incumbent: LFM2.5-2.6B |
|---|---|---|
| Parameters | 2.52B total, 1.98B non-embedding | 2.6B |
| License | **Apache-2.0** | LFM Open License v1.0, USD 10M revenue cap |
| Q4_K_M GGUF | **1.56 GB** | 1.67 GB |
| Context | 131,072 | 128,000 |
| Architecture | Stock `LlamaForCausalLM`, 42 layers, GQA (16 Q heads, 2 KV heads) | Liquid hybrid |
| Official GGUF repo | `openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B-GGUF`, ungated (Q4_K_M 1.56 GB, Q8_0 2.68 GB, F16 5.04 GB) | `LiquidAI/LFM2.5-2.6B-GGUF`, ungated |
| Tool calling | Supported, XML-style; vendor recommends SGLang as backend | Verified locally, pythonic spans |
| Multimodal | No, text only | No |
| Origin | China | USA |
| Released | 2026-09-07 | 2026-08-04 |

**Why it earns a slot.** The catalog's smallest agentic entry is the only LLM in the roster carrying a revenue-conditioned commercial license. Every user on a CPU-only host or a sub-4 GB GPU is routed to it by `recommended.json`, so the license constraint applies precisely to the users with the least ability to choose otherwise. MiniCPM5-2B removes that constraint at a smaller download with equal context.

**Why the architecture note matters more than the benchmarks.** The card claims 2B-class open SOTA at an average of 53.9 across its comparison set, which includes LFM2.5-2.6B, Qwen3.5-2B, and Gemma-4-E2B directly. Those are vendor-reported numbers and per the catalog's own convention they may not appear in card copy until locally reproduced. What is verifiable and load-bearing is the architecture: stock `LlamaForCausalLM` with no custom kernels and no model-code fork. That is what makes the Ollama and llama.cpp path predictable, and it is why this is a low-integration-risk addition rather than another Muse-Glimmer-style minimum-Ollama-version negotiation.

**The real integration work is the tool-call parser, not the download.** Nexus has format-specific parsers in `modules/coding/llm/ToolCallFormat.ts` (`gemma4-xml`, `lfm-pythonic`). MiniCPM5 emits XML-style tool calls but against a vendor-recommended SGLang backend, not Ollama. Whether the Ollama GGUF path produces spans the existing XML parser accepts is unknown and must be measured, not assumed. This is gate G1 in Section 7.

## Section 4: Candidate 2 -- the Mistral lineup

Four Mistral options were assessed against the 24 GB ceiling and the first-party-GGUF bar.

| Model | Params | License | Official GGUF | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ministral 3 14B Instruct** | 14B dense | Apache-2.0 | **Yes**, `mistralai/Ministral-3-14B-Instruct-2512-GGUF`, ungated | **ADOPT, opt-in** |
| Mistral Small 4 | 119B MoE | Apache-2.0 | Community only | REJECT |
| Devstral Small 2 | 24B dense | Apache-2.0 | Not found; community re-uploads only | REJECT |
| Ministral 3 3B / 8B | 3B / 8B | Apache-2.0 | Yes | REJECT, redundant |

### 4.1 Ministral 3 14B Instruct -- ADOPT (opt-in, not a tier default)

Q4_K_M at 8.24 GB, Apache-2.0, 256K context, vision capable, native function calling and JSON output, official ungated `mistralai` GGUF repo, released December 2025 as part of the Ministral 3 family.

It slots against `gemma-4-12b-it-gguf` (7.6 GB, 11 GB VRAM, Gemma Terms of Use). Direct comparison: Ministral is slightly larger on disk, comparable on context (256K vs 262K), also multimodal, and Apache-2.0 instead of use-restricted. It is the only credible license-clean option in that band.

**Why opt-in rather than a tier default.** `gemma-4-12b-it-gguf` is the pre-ticked chat and agentic pick on both the 12 and 16 GB tiers, and displacing it is a product decision with field consequences the v2.4 field-testing gate has not cleared. Ship Ministral 3 14B as an untagged catalog entry first, let it accumulate real use, and revisit the tier default in a later cycle with evidence. Adding a catalog row is reversible; changing what every 12 GB machine downloads on first install is not.

**Honest caveat.** The license argument is the main argument. Gemma Terms of Use is a use-restriction and prohibited-use policy, not a revenue cap, and the catalog already ships it as the primary chat default without incident. This is a smaller problem than the LFM revenue cap that MiniCPM5-2B solves. Ministral 3 14B is worth adding, but it is a second-priority item, and if only one model ships in v2.4.10 it should be MiniCPM5-2B.

### 4.2 Mistral Small 4 -- REJECT

A 119B-parameter MoE with a 262,144 token context. A Q4_K_M quantization lands around 60 to 70 GB. The catalog's largest entry is 25 GB and its highest hardware tier is 24 GB VRAM. This is not a close call: no Nexus target machine can run it, and adding an entry that is permanently over budget on every tier would only fill the incompatible bucket at the bottom of both lists.

### 4.3 Devstral Small 2 -- REJECT

24B, Apache-2.0, 128K context, released December 2025, and genuinely strong at software engineering tasks. Two reasons it does not make the cut.

First, redundancy: `qwen3-coder:30b` already holds the 20 GB coding-agentic slot under the same Apache-2.0 license, and `gpt-oss:20b` holds 16 GB. Devstral 2 would compete for one of those slots rather than fill an empty one.

Second, and decisive: the search surfaced only community GGUF re-uploads (`unsloth/*`), not a first-party `mistralai` GGUF repository. The catalog has an explicit named invariant against exactly this class of pull target, because the Unsloth `hf.co` GGUF reference is a known Ollama manifest failure that downloads fully then errors HTTP 400. Routing a new entry through the same path would reintroduce the v1.13.0 defect the invariant exists to prevent.

If Mistral publishes a first-party Devstral 2 GGUF, this rejection should be revisited on the redundancy question alone.

### 4.4 Ministral 3 3B and 8B -- REJECT

3B overlaps MiniCPM5-2B and `qwen3.5:4b` without beating either on license or size. 8B overlaps `qwen3.5:9b`, which is already Apache-2.0 with a longer context. Adding both would grow the Agentic tab by two rows that a user has no clear basis for choosing between. Catalog surface area is a real cost on a tab users scan during installation.

## Section 5: Sorting -- CORRECTED

> **This section was wrong when first written.** It claimed the installer and Settings sort identically and that placement is a pure function of `tags` and `releaseDate`. The comparator is indeed shared. The tier input is not, and that difference decides whether a promoted model appears promoted to the user. The original claim and its correction are both kept below, because the mistake is instructive: a shared comparator with a dual-asserted fixture looked like proof of parity, and it only ever proved parity of the comparison function, never of its inputs.

### 5.1 What is actually shared

**One comparator, implemented twice, asserted against one fixture:**

| Surface | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Desktop Settings / Models | `core/registry/modelDisplayPolicy.ts` -> `settingsModelDisplayOrder` |
| Installer typed catalog | `scripts/installer/src/nexus_installer/catalog_tab_sort.py` -> `canonical_display_order` |
| Shared fixture | `core/registry/model-display-order.fixture.json` |
| Parity tests | `desktop/tests/catalogTabs.test.ts` and `scripts/installer/tests/test_catalog_tab_sort.py` |

The fixture carries both `expectedInstaller` and `expectedSettings` orderings and both test suites assert against it, so the two surfaces cannot drift without a test failing.

**The ordering rule** (`compareModelDisplayRows`), applied in sequence:

1. Compatible before incompatible (incompatible means `vramGB` exceeds host VRAM, or no GPU)
2. Tier: `required` -> `recommended` -> `compatible` (read from `tags`)
3. `releaseDate` descending, newest first
4. Display name, then id, as deterministic tiebreakers

Settings applies one additional bucket ahead of all of that: downloaded models first. That bucket is captured in the fixture, and it means a Settings observation of ordering must be made with the model absent from the local store.

### 5.2 What is NOT shared, and why it matters

The two surfaces feed that comparator different `tags`.

- **Desktop Settings** passes the catalog row through unchanged. `modelDisplayPolicy.ts` `modelDisplayTier` reads `row.tags` and returns `recommended` when the catalog row carries it.
- **The installer discards the catalog's tags.** `scripts/installer/src/nexus_installer/pages/typed_catalog.py` `_catalog_model_sort_row` opens with `tags: list[str] = []`, then appends `"required"` from `model.is_required` or `"recommended"` from membership in the `recommended.json` tier defaults. A `recommended` tag in `catalog.json` never reaches the installer's comparator. The same file passes `recommended=model.id in defaults` for the Recommended pill.

**Consequence.** Tagging a model `recommended` in `catalog.json` alone promotes it in Settings and does nothing in the installer. Doing that to MiniCPM5-2B would ship a user-visible contradiction: Settings ranking it above LFM2.5-2.6B while the installer keeps LFM2.5 pre-ticked and pill-badged. Promotion has to change **both** inputs, and `recommended.json` is a first-install behavior change.

Two smaller parity boundaries, recorded for accuracy: `catalog_tab_sort.is_incompatible` also fails a row on `requiredRamGB` and hides rows below `hideBelowVramGB`, and `modelDisplayPolicy` has neither term. Neither fires for these candidates, but "one rule" was an overstatement there too.

### 5.3 Where each candidate lands

`releaseDate` and `vramGB` are both load-bearing for position, and neither is measured yet.

- **MiniCPM5-2B**, released 2026-09-07, is newer than every entry in the catalog (the current newest is `lfm2.5:2.6b` at 2026-08-04). Untagged, it sorts to the top of the compatible block on the Agentic tab on both surfaces. Promoted, it leads the recommended block in Settings and, without a `recommended.json` change, stays below LFM2.5 in the installer.
- **Ministral 3 14B**, released 2025-12-02, untagged, sorts after every recommended-tagged Chat row (tier precedes date), then among the untagged rows by date.

`releaseDate` must be pinned from repository history rather than announcement coverage, which is gate G3. `vramGB` is a Nexus-side compatibility threshold that is neither measured nor vendor-documented, and it drives the compatible/incompatible partition, so how it is derived must be recorded rather than asserted.

## Section 6: Policy assessment (MCP registry decision tree)

Both candidates are **bucket 1, local-only**. They are open-weight models pulled at install time from ungated first-party Hugging Face repositories and run locally through Ollama or llama.cpp. No outbound service, no vendor API, no search-as-service, embeddings-as-service, or generation-as-service. Nexus does not redistribute weights; it references first-party repos and pins per-file SHA256.

No reverse-engineering assessment applies: nothing is being adopted from an external codebase. These are model references, not code.

**License posture.** Both are Apache-2.0, which is strictly better than the two constrained licenses currently in the roster (LFM Open License v1.0 and Gemma Terms of Use). Adopting both moves the catalog toward fewer use-restricted entries, which is the substantive product argument running through this whole report.

## Section 7: Blocking verification gates

None of the three can be satisfied from a model card. All require running the models locally.

**G1 -- MiniCPM5-2B tool-call format (blocks the `recommended` tag).**
Pull the Q4_K_M GGUF through Ollama, run the Nexus agentic harness against it, and determine whether the emitted XML-style tool calls parse under the existing `gemma4-xml` parser in `modules/coding/llm/ToolCallFormat.ts` or need a new `minicpm5-xml` variant. The vendor recommends SGLang for tool calls, which is not the Nexus path, so the Ollama GGUF behavior is genuinely unknown. Record the result as a `toolCallingBenchmark` entry the way `lfm2.5:2.6b` does. Until this passes, the entry ships untagged with `toolCallingVerified: false`.

**G2 -- SHA256 pins for both entries.**
`weights.files[].sha256` must be the hash of the actually pulled blob. These cannot be fabricated or inferred. Pull, hash, pin, and confirm the invariants in `catalog_invariants.py` still pass. `scripts/installer/build/pin-hf-weights.py` exists for this.

**G3 -- Release dates confirmed against repo history.**
MiniCPM5-2B is reported as 2026-09-07 and Ministral 3 as 2025-12-02 (the `2512` in the repo name). Because `releaseDate` determines sort position, confirm both against the Hugging Face repository commit history rather than announcement coverage.

**Non-blocking but required before ship:** card copy for both entries must avoid vendor benchmark numbers and suite names, per the existing convention enforced for LFM and Muse-Glimmer.

## Section 8: Risks

**R1 -- Displacing the CPU-tier agentic default is a field-visible change.**
If MiniCPM5-2B eventually takes the `cpu` and `8` tier agentic default from LFM2.5-2.6B in `recommended.json`, every CPU-only and low-VRAM first install changes what it downloads. The v2.4 release is already gated on field testing. Recommendation: ship the catalog entry in v2.4.10 and defer the `recommended.json` tier-default change to a separate, later decision backed by G1 evidence and field use.

**R2 -- Community GGUF re-uploads are a live confusion risk.**
MiniCPM5-2B has roughly 20 third-party GGUF and quantization repos, including abliterated variants. Only `openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B-GGUF` may be referenced. The same applies to Ministral: only `mistralai/*`. This is the exact failure mode the existing Unsloth invariant was written for.

**R3 -- Origin concentration.**
Adding MiniCPM5-2B (China) alongside the three Qwen entries makes four of the small-and-mid agentic slots China-origin. The catalog tracks `origin` as a first-class field, which implies it is a user-facing consideration. Ministral 3 14B (EU) partly offsets this. Flagging it as a product question, not a technical objection.

**R4 -- Catalog surface growth.**
Two more rows on tabs users scan during installation. Mitigated by the fact that MiniCPM5-2B is intended to eventually replace rather than sit beside LFM in the tier defaults, and by rejecting the four redundant Mistral options.

## Section 9: Prioritized adoption plan

> **Superseded.** The table below is the original assessment. It underestimated the work in two ways that only surfaced during plan review, and its decisions were revised. The authoritative sequence is now [the v2.4.10 adoption plan](../plans/v2.4.10-adoption-minicpm5-mistral-models.md). Both are kept: the original shows what a card-level comparison could see, the revision shows what reading the code changed.

| # | Item | Value | Effort | Gates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Add `minicpm5:2b` to `catalog.json` as an untagged Agentic entry | **High** | S | G2, G3 |
| 2 | Run the tool-call format check; add a parser variant if needed | **High** | M | G1 |
| 3 | Promote `minicpm5:2b` to `recommended` once G1 passes | **High** | XS | G1 |
| 4 | Add `ministral3:14b` to `catalog.json` as an untagged entry | Medium | S | G2, G3 |
| 5 | Extend `catalog_invariants.py` with license-label and pin contracts | Medium | S | -- |
| 6 | Revisit the `cpu` / `8` tier agentic default after field evidence | Medium | XS | G1, R1 |
| 7 | Sorting | No change | -- | **Wrong, see Section 5** |

**What the plan changed, and why:**

- **Item 7 was wrong.** Sorting parity is not free (Section 5.2). Promotion must edit `recommended.json` as well as `catalog.json`, or the surfaces disagree.
- **Item 6 stopped being deferrable.** Because the installer derives `recommended` from `recommended.json`, the tier-default edit IS the promotion, not a follow-up to it. Deferring it would have promoted the model in Settings only and delivered the license benefit to nobody who was actually being routed to LFM2.5. The plan now changes both tier inputs together, gated on the G1 evidence.
- **A whole registry was missing from items 1 to 3.** `core/registry/ModelCatalog.ts` and its `models.json` mirror carry `promptFormat` and `toolFormat` per model, separate from `catalog.json`. `lfm2.5:2.6b` is in both. Without an entry there, an unknown family falls through `modelAliases.ts` to a `llama3-json` default, so a catalog-only adoption ships an agentic model on a silently wrong parser. The plan registers the runtime before touching the catalog.
- **Item 2 was too narrow.** It assumed the check was against `gemma4-xml`. Five parsers exist, and for an XML-style emitter on stock `LlamaForCausalLM` the likelier match is the existing `qwen-json`. Probing one of five and finding it fails would have made a new parser look necessary. The plan probes all five, and names any new parser after the wire grammar rather than the model.
- **Item 4 was cut.** Ministral 3 14B is deferred to a known gap. Its only argument is license cleanliness against Gemma Terms of Use, which the catalog already ships as the primary chat default without incident, and it costs an 8.24 GB download plus an entry, an invariant, and four assertions for an untagged row.
- **Item 5 grew slightly.** Two rules described as catalog-wide (placeholder-SHA rejection, forbidden benchmark copy) are reachable only through hardcoded per-id blocks. Adding two more blocks would leave the gap open for the next entry, so the plan hoists both into the main validation loop.

## Section 10: What to preserve

The investigation surfaced three pieces of existing catalog machinery that answered questions before they had to be asked, and that should not be eroded:

1. **The shared comparator with a dual-asserted fixture, and the lesson about its limits.** Keeping one comparison function in sync across the Python and TypeScript halves of the product is genuinely valuable and worth reusing. The lesson is that it proves less than it appears to: the fixture asserts the comparator, and both surfaces passed it while feeding that comparator different `tags`. A parity mechanism should cover a rule's inputs, not only its logic. Extending the fixture to cover tier derivation would close that.
2. **`catalog_invariants.py` as a named-defect registry.** It does not encode abstract rules, it encodes specific past failures with the id, the broken target, and the symptom. It is what turned the Devstral 2 assessment from a guess into a decision.
3. **The no-vendor-benchmarks-in-card-copy convention.** It kept the MiniCPM5 "2B-class SOTA, average 53.9" claim out of the recommendation and forced the argument onto verifiable ground (license, size, architecture, provenance), which is where it belongs.
